What an Upstream Sound-Event Tag Costs: Claim, Packaging, and Declared Confidence

Seunghun Oh · Hyunjung Chung · Ganghyeon Jeong · Unsang Park
Sogang University · Corresponding author: Unsang Park

When an upstream sound-event tag reaches a multimodal model, what matters most: the claim, how it is packaged, or its declared confidence? We separate these effects and measure their costs in a real detector–reader cascade.

GitHub RepositoryManuscript SourceExplore Protocol
Research question

One transmitted tag, three distinct components.

A detector may describe its decision as a sentence, even when the recording provides conflicting evidence. The resulting text mixes the claim, an implicit or explicit statement of confidence, and the way the message is formatted. We separate what can be tested one factor at a time from what must be treated as a bundled presentation change.

01 · CLAIM

What the tag asserts

Claim: “The clip does not contain the sound of X.”

Compare claims while keeping the signal and question fixed.

Method

From matched message interventions to a calibrated cascade.

Six frozen audio and audio–visual reader models are tested on sound events and constructed diagnostic attributes. A metadata-free perceptual-eligibility gate and answer-order audit separate robust cells from sensitivity analyses.

01 · Input signalVGGSound sounds and matched diagnostic questions
02 · Controlled tagClaim insertion, field name, and confidence levels
03 · Reader responseAccuracy, override probability, and logit margins
04 · Real cascadeCLAP forwarding, error repair, and break-even

Reader models: Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, VideoLLaMA2.1, Qwen2-Audio, Audio Flamingo 3 and Nemotron 3 Nano Omni.

Key findings

The components do not have equal effects.

Claim first

The forwarded assertion matters most.

A false claim causes the largest adverse margin step in most evaluated controlled condition comparisons.

2.1–3.9×

Confidence vs. Priority

The high-to-low claim-aligned logit-margin swing is larger with an epistemic confidence field. This ratio is not an accuracy multiplier.

−6.6 to −11.1 pp

Bare forwarding can reduce accuracy.

Across the four order-robust CLAP cascade cells, forwarding the tag as plain text loses accuracy compared with omitting it.

Break-even

The upstream detector's accuracy matters.

Improving detector quality can change whether forwarding helps. Outcomes depend on detector, reader, dataset and serialization.

Interpretation: Message packaging changes several properties at once, and the reported gains and losses are conditional on the tested cells. The results do not establish a universal policy for all detectors or readers.
